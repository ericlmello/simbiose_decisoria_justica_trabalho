Decision Symbiosis

Instead of trying to open the technology black box, we govern the human decision. A model that applies from BI dashboards to predictive models.

The central tension: automation vs. human oversight Technical explainability of algorithmic systems is hard to achieve. Shifting the focus from "system governance" to "decision governance" solves this problem and puts the human back at the center of the process.

Paradigm
From Artificial Intelligence to Augmented Intelligence: the machine extends human capacity, it does not replace it.
Focus
Decision Governance instead of attempting System Governance.
Scope
Technology-agnostic: applies to spreadsheets, traditional BI dashboards or Generative AI.
Method and Rule
Symbiosis is the working method; Governance is the validation rule.

Decision Symbiosis

When a court adopts a technological system, the governance literature focuses exhaustively on trying to open the algorithm "black box". The problem is that, with Generative AI and complex models, full technical explainability is nearly impossible. The GSD-ISD model takes a pragmatic stance: instead of trying to govern the system, we should govern the decisions made with the system.

This approach removes the difficulty of technical explainability and reinforces human responsibility, in line with the legal nature of public administration. In a court, every decision requires legal grounds; technology is only the means, but final authorship is non-transferable and belongs exclusively to a human (judge, director or civil servant).

Technology Neutrality, Bias and Business Focus

Extending this model to traditional systems, such as Business Intelligence (BI) dashboards and decision support systems, is not only possible but strategic, for the following reasons:

  • Technology-agnostic governance: The court builds a future-proof framework. Whether the tool is ChatGPT, a predictive model, a Power BI dashboard or an Excel spreadsheet, the rule applies to the impact of the final decision. This avoids rewriting policies with every new innovation.
  • Mitigating automation bias in any environment: "Anchoring bias" is not exclusive to AI. A poorly built BI chart, or one fed with unvalidated data, can lead to a serious error. Governing the decision obliges the user to validate the information, however simple the source system may be.
  • Focus on the business, not only on IT: Models fail when they become an "IT checklist". Governing the decision places responsibility on the business unit that actually uses the dashboard, ensuring the system serves its practical purpose.

Augmented Intelligence: Symbiosis as the Engine

In this proposal, technology is not seen as a replacement but as Augmented Intelligence, that is, an extension of human capacity. The machine brings the raw power to process massive volumes of data, identify patterns or draft documents at scale; the human brings empathy, judgment, a sense of fairness and the legal seal of approval.

Decision Symbiosis is the method (how the work is done); decision governance is the framework that ensures the human has truly taken authorship, preventing the mere act of "pressing approve without reading".

The GSD-ISD model · specified and verified

Decision Sensitivity Index (ISD)

Classifies each use of the system by risk, combining impact on rights and irreversibility. Oversight is applied to the decision and is proportional to risk.

Three control layers

Separates those who operate, those who oversee and those who audit, so that control falls on the decision chain. The audit is automated, internal and run by sprints, sampling the classification, the record sheet and the integrity of the audit trail.

Protocol A1–A6

A core of six criteria required from the pilot onward, drawn from a nine-criteria design across four compliance pillars. The remaining three (A7 to A9) are specified as an institutional maturity extension. Designed for business managers, without software development jargon.

Decision chain record

The Record Sheet logs the steps and the reasons, materializing the non-transferable human responsibility (legal seal of approval) required by the court regulatory framework.

Architecture extensions · under development and implemented

Design friction in the interface under research

Cognitive forcing functions and micro-boundaries only in high-risk tasks, mitigating automation bias in BI or AI without stiffening the routine.

Practicability and shadow AI under research

When the audited channel is overly bureaucratic, staff look for undeclared external solutions (loose spreadsheets, generic AI). Practicability is vital.

Data governance at the source implemented

A dashboard with a half-finished load leads to error. Validation between steps and deviation detection separate complete data from partial data at the BI source.

Tracking and goal control implemented

Each run of the pipeline receives a unique identifier, chained by an integrity check. The indicators compared with the historical pattern come from this trail. Trend projection is the next step, not yet implemented.

Phase 0 · baseline diagnosis, proposal not yet executed

Before applying any friction, the proposed pilot measures the path of least resistance. Automation bias is documented, but its prevalence varies by context: friction applied without a diagnosis may be excessive, pushing use toward external tools, or insufficient, leaving the bias unaddressed. For this reason, the two weeks that precede the 12-week pilot, organized in three sprints, are used to measure current decision-making behavior and calibrate friction to what is observed.

Phase 0 · 1

Observe

Non-invasive observation of 50 to 100 decisions, with no intervention, and review of 200 to 300 prior records, where they exist.

Phase 0 · 2

Listen

Semi-structured interviews with 6 to 8 judges and clerks about uncertainty, trust in the recommendation and the practicability of the protocol.

Phase 0 · 3

Calibrate

Segmentation by decision type and by unit, never by person, and a plan that indicates where to apply friction and how strongly.

Phase 0 · 4

Compare

Edit rate, decision time and consultation of the data source, measured beforehand, are measured again in the sprints, allowing a before-and-after comparison. Phase 0 also measures quality by week of the month, to test whether it drops near the end of the period.

Because the institutional audit trail does not capture the conduct of whoever decides, Phase 0 is a separate study, with informed consent and aggregated results.

The Decision Symbiosis cycle

After Phase 0, the four pillars above are organized into six steps, in which the human validates the system and governance validates the human. Step 6 reopens step 1: the cycle is iterative.

Step 1

Govern the use of the dashboard

The committee defines which decisions supported by the dashboard or by AI require a checkpoint before acting, combining probability and impact. Management operates the cognitive forcing screen that applies this rule.

Step 2

Calculate decision risk

Classifies decisions made from the dashboard by impact on rights and reversibility (ISD), regardless of the technology adopted.

Step 3

Validate the data

Checks each load before releasing the next, restarts failures and detects deviations, mitigating bias caused by input errors.

Step 4

Document and Trace

Automatically records each run and chains it with an integrity check. Human intervention requires an identified person responsible and a reason. Every decision supported by the system has the signing person identified, with name and position; monitoring indicators, however, are by unit, never by person.

Step 5

Map the upstream process

Shows the data steps and earlier AI uses that produced the indicator, providing context for human judgment.

Step 6

Evaluate and Project (Predictive Dashboard)

Compares indicators with goals, shows progress and anticipates bottlenecks, subjecting the AI to the user scrutiny. It also gathers the quality-loss risk indicators and sprint monitoring. Steps 1 to 5 are implemented as a proof of concept; the predictive component is a later phase.

Governance, management and audit are distinct roles

Governance, exercised by the committee, defines where verification is mandatory, sets the goals and decides on alerts every sprint. Management operates the interface and the data routines day to day. The internal automated audit is distinct from both, samples every sprint, reports to the committee and has the artifact and the process as its object, never the merits of the decision or the conduct of whoever decides. It checks the risk classification, the integrity of the audit trail and the completeness of the record sheet, ensuring that the decision chain leaves an auditable and irrefutable trace.

Probability × impact

The ISD measures the impact of the decision on rights and its irreversibility. To it is added the probability of quality loss, rated low, medium or high by six context indicators tracked by unit, never by judge. Crossed with impact, the two dimensions form a risk matrix, which brings forward the reassessment of high-risk uses in high-probability units. Thresholds are calibrated in Phase 0, and the alert triggers support from management, not enforcement.

Quality indicators are preventive, since they act on the condition that favors bias before quality loss sets in. The audit trail and anomaly flagging are detective. Conceptually aligned with the IIA Three Lines Model (2020).

Step 6: Integrated Dashboard and Augmented Intelligence

The trail created for auditing makes the oversight regime itself measurable. From it come, with no parallel data collection and no extra input from the operator, six indicators, one per step of the cycle: compliance (high-risk decisions with reasoning recorded), classification (uses classified by ISD with reassessment up to date), integrity (loads successfully validated), recovery (average time to fix failures), traceability (lineage mapped and accessible) and alerts (false positives in predictive alerts, from the pilot of the predictive module). Reference goals are calibrated against the Phase 0 baseline. Measurement stops being a separate project and becomes a by-product of the record.

The same base supports the predictive opportunity. Court dashboards today are descriptive: they show what has already happened, without anticipating case backlog build-up or the risk of missing a goal. Projecting trends is the next step, and it depends on what comes before, because a predictive model over unvalidated data projects the error with more confidence. The judge or manager submits the projection to ISD scrutiny before acting.

The decision-behavior indicators measured in Phase 0 (edit rate, decision time and consultation of the source) reappear in the pilot as a benchmark, to show whether friction produced the expected effect.

Integrated interface

The artifacts make up a single interface. Beside each indicator, the side panel shows the source and the data update, and the load control panel shows load validation. In high-risk decisions, the cognitive forcing screen asks for the justification. At the top, the alert card consolidates deviations and the backlog projection, a predictive extension of today descriptive dashboards. The committee defines where to verify and decides on alerts, and management operates the interface.

Quality indicators, with qualitative goals and trend reading within each unit, never in a ranking, coexist with the quality-loss risk indicators, which flag deterioration before it sets in. The same dashboard is reused in the following sprints to monitor the pilot. Control is done by sprints, with a weekly reading of the behavior indicators, a monthly trend of quality and risk and a projection updated every sprint.

Anticipated objections

The questions a governance committee asks first, and the answers the design already contains.

Question 1

Does this watch the civil servant or the judge?

Recording is by organizational unit, never by person. The trail documents technical execution and the unit acts, not the behavior of whoever decides.

Question 2

Does this interfere with the decision?

The checkpoint alerts and asks for a record, but neither blocks nor steers the choice. The reasoning required by art. 93, IX, of the Brazilian Constitution is not replaced by any element of the interface.

Question 3

Does this slow the work down?

Friction costs seconds and applies only to high-risk decisions. In the data steps, automation reduces the time now spent on manual load checks.

Question 4

Why measure before acting?

Without a baseline, there is no way to know whether friction worked or whether it was proportional to the problem. Phase 0 observes without intervening, and its results follow the rule of no individual metrics.
