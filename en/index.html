Decision Symbiosis

A data governance area to prevent the loss of quality in decisions supported by dashboards and AI, especially at the end of goal periods. Instead of opening the technology black box, we govern the decision.

The committee's dilemma: every decision vs. volume and independence To protect quality, the committee would have to assess the risk of every decision, which is impossible given the volume and would encroach on judicial independence. The way out is to assess risk by decision type, with probability by unit and period, and to bring only the exceptions to the committee, through a Data Governance Area.

Paradigm
From Artificial Intelligence to Augmented Intelligence: the machine extends human capacity, it does not replace it.
Focus
Decision Governance instead of attempting System Governance.
Scope
Technology-agnostic: applies to spreadsheets, traditional BI dashboards or Generative AI.
Method and Rule
Symbiosis is the working method; Governance is the validation rule.

The problem and the proposal

Goals measured on fixed dates concentrate decisions in the final weeks of each period, and that is when automation bias weighs most. Under deadline pressure, decision-makers tend to accept what the dashboard or the suggested draft displays without checking where the data comes from (Parasuraman; Manzey, 2010). In a fictitious court, weekly output rises from 58 to 118 decisions in the last week of the month, and adequate reasoning in the sample falls from 91% to 72%. The monthly average hides the pattern, and a dashboard that shows only productivity records the result as good performance.

The committee's dilemma. To protect quality, the committee would have to assess the risk of every decision, but reviewing decision by decision is impossible given the volume and would encroach on judicial independence. Assessing only the system or the project, once, at approval, misses the fact that the same decision becomes riskier in the last week of the month. The way out is to assess risk by decision type, combining impact, which is stable, with the probability of quality loss, which varies by unit and by period, and to bring only the exceptions to the committee.

The opportunity. As the largest labor court in Brazil, TRT-2 multiplies any data or decision error by its volume. CNJ Resolution 615/2025 requires risk classification, impact assessment, auditing and monitoring of AI solutions, and establishing governance of data and decisions before creating and using AI at scale costs less than fixing it afterwards.

Data Governance Area

Sits on the committee and acts as the second line of the Three Lines Model. It is responsible for the quality, provenance and lineage of dashboard data, maintains the classification of decision types and the indicators, and brings only the exceptions to the committee. Through data stewards, it bridges project demands and the business area, which keeps ownership of the demand; execution stays with IT.

Risk by decision type

CNJ Resolution 615/2025 classifies solutions, and project approval assesses risk once. Here risk belongs to the decision type, such as a release of funds or a reallocation of the workforce, and it rises when the unit enters the closing week.

Preventive and predictive indicators

Six context indicators estimate the probability of quality loss, and the unit's weekly projection anticipates the drop before the period closes. The alert triggers management support, not enforcement.

No blocking on screen

The requirements fall on the data, the reasoning sample and the unit's management, not on the decision-maker's screen, and this applies equally to descriptive dashboards and to AI solutions.

Decision Symbiosis

When a court adopts a technological system, the governance literature focuses exhaustively on trying to open the algorithm "black box". The problem is that, with Generative AI and complex models, full technical explainability is nearly impossible. The GSD-ISD model takes a pragmatic stance: instead of trying to govern the system, we should govern the decisions made with the system.

This approach removes the difficulty of technical explainability and reinforces human responsibility, in line with the legal nature of public administration. In a court, every decision requires legal grounds; technology is only the means, but final authorship is non-transferable and belongs exclusively to a human (judge, director or civil servant).

Technology Neutrality, Bias and Business Focus

Extending this model to traditional systems, such as Business Intelligence (BI) dashboards and decision support systems, is not only possible but strategic, for the following reasons:

  • Technology-agnostic governance: The court builds a future-proof framework. Whether the tool is ChatGPT, a predictive model, a Power BI dashboard or an Excel spreadsheet, the rule applies to the impact of the final decision. This avoids rewriting policies with every new innovation.
  • Mitigating automation bias in any environment: "Anchoring bias" is not exclusive to AI. A poorly built BI chart, or one fed with unvalidated data, can lead to a serious error. Governing the decision obliges the user to validate the information, however simple the source system may be.
  • Focus on the business, not only on IT: Models fail when they become an "IT checklist". Governing the decision places responsibility on the business unit that actually uses the dashboard, ensuring the system serves its practical purpose.

Augmented Intelligence: Symbiosis as the Engine

In this proposal, technology is not seen as a replacement but as Augmented Intelligence, that is, an extension of human capacity. The machine brings the raw power to process massive volumes of data, identify patterns or draft documents at scale; the human brings empathy, judgment, a sense of fairness and the legal seal of approval.

Decision Symbiosis is the method (how the work is done); decision governance is the framework that ensures the human has truly taken authorship, preventing the mere act of "pressing approve without reading".

The GSD-ISD model · specified and verified

Decision Sensitivity Index (ISD)

Classifies each decision type by risk, combining impact on rights and irreversibility. Oversight is applied to the decision and is proportional to risk.

Three control layers

Separates those who operate, those who govern and those who audit. The Data Governance Area, a member of the committee, runs the automated audit every sprint, sampling the classification, the record sheet and the integrity of the audit trail, while the court's internal audit remains in the third line.

Protocol A1–A6

A core of six criteria required from the pilot onward, drawn from a nine-criteria design across four compliance pillars. The remaining three (A7 to A9) are specified as an institutional maturity extension. Designed for business managers, without software development jargon.

Decision chain record

The Record Sheet logs the steps and the reasons, materializing the non-transferable human responsibility (legal seal of approval) required by the court regulatory framework.

Practicability and shadow AI under research

When the audited channel is overly bureaucratic, staff look for undeclared external solutions (loose spreadsheets, generic AI). Practicability is vital.

Data governance at the source implemented

A dashboard with a half-finished load leads to error. Validation between steps and deviation detection separate complete data from partial data at the BI source.

Tracking and goal control implemented

Each run of the pipeline receives a unique identifier, chained by an integrity check. The indicators compared with the historical pattern come from this trail. Trend projection is the next step, not yet implemented.

Phase 0 · baseline diagnosis, proposal not yet executed

Before setting requirements and thresholds, the proposed pilot measures how decisions are made today. Automation bias is documented, but its prevalence varies by context, and controls set without a diagnosis may be excessive or insufficient. For this reason, the two weeks that precede the 12-week pilot, organized in three sprints, are used to measure current quality, including by week of the month, and to calibrate the thresholds.

Phase 0 · 1

Observe

Non-invasive observation of 50 to 100 decisions, with no intervention, and review of 200 to 300 prior records, where they exist.

Phase 0 · 2

Listen

Semi-structured interviews with 6 to 8 judges and clerks about uncertainty, trust in the recommendation and the practicability of the protocol.

Phase 0 · 3

Calibrate

Segmentation by decision type and by unit, never by person, and the initial classification of decision types, with the thresholds for the preventive and predictive indicators.

Phase 0 · 4

Compare

Adequate reasoning in a sample and the usage indicators (edit rate, time to confirmation and consultation of the data source), measured beforehand, are measured again in the sprints. Phase 0 also measures quality by week of the month, to test whether it drops near the end of the period.

Because the institutional audit trail does not capture the conduct of whoever decides, Phase 0 is a separate study, with informed consent and aggregated results.

The Decision Symbiosis cycle

After Phase 0, the four pillars above are organized into six steps, in which the human validates the system and governance validates the human. Step 6 reopens step 1: the cycle is iterative.

Step 1

Govern the use of the dashboard

The committee defines, by decision type, the requirements of each risk layer, combining probability and impact. The Data Governance Area records the rule in the Record Sheet, and nothing on the screen blocks or conditions the decision.

Step 2

Calculate decision risk

Classifies the decision types made from the dashboard or from AI by impact on rights and reversibility (ISD) and crosses them with the unit's probability of quality loss, regardless of the technology adopted.

Step 3

Validate the data

Checks each load before releasing the next, restarts failures and detects deviations, mitigating bias caused by input errors.

Step 4

Document and Trace

Automatically records each run and chains it with an integrity check. Human intervention requires an identified person responsible and a reason. Every decision supported by the system has the signing person identified, with name and position; monitoring indicators, however, are by unit, never by person.

Step 5

Map the upstream process

Shows the data steps and earlier AI uses that produced the indicator, providing context for human judgment.

Step 6

Evaluate and Project (Predictive Dashboard)

Compares indicators with goals, projects quality and volume up to the close of the period and the backlog for the following months, and anticipates risk by unit, subjecting the projection to the committee's scrutiny. Steps 1 to 5 are implemented as a proof of concept; the predictive component is a later phase.

Governance, management and audit are distinct roles

Governance, exercised by the committee, sets the requirements for each layer, sets the goals and decides on exceptions every sprint. Management operates the interface and the data routines day to day. The Data Governance Area sits on the committee and links project demands to the business area: through data stewards appointed by the user areas, it translates each need into data and quality requirements, classifies with the requesting area the decision types the project will support, and returns the indicators in management language. The demand and the outcome remain with the business area, and execution with IT. As the second line, it maintains the data, the indicators and the classification of decision types and runs the automated audit every sprint, which checks the risk classification, the integrity of the audit trail and the completeness of the record sheet, without examining the merits of the decision or the conduct of whoever decides. The court's internal audit unit remains in the third line (CNJ Resolution 309/2020) and may use the trail as input without losing its independence.

Probability × impact and predictive indicators

The ISD measures the impact of the decision on rights and its irreversibility. To it is added the probability of quality loss, rated low, medium or high by six context indicators tracked by unit, never by judge. Crossed with impact, the two dimensions form a risk matrix, which brings forward the reassessment of high-risk decision types in high-probability units. The predictive indicators project, from the unit's own weekly series, quality and volume up to the close of the period and trigger the alert before the critical week, with reliability measured by hit rate and false positives. Thresholds are calibrated in Phase 0, and the alert triggers support from management, not enforcement.

Controls by type

Preventive

  • Classification by decision type, by impact and probability
  • Preconditions on data representativeness and explainability
  • Load validation and distribution of the workload across the period
  • Preventive quality-loss risk indicators, read by trend and with no ranking
  • Predictive indicators: projection of quality, volume and backlog up to the close
  • Training

Detective

  • Record sheet and immutable audit trail
  • Reasoning sampling and aggregated usage indicators
  • Anomaly flagging
  • Alert and trend

Corrective

  • Committee decision every sprint (expand, adjust or suspend)
  • Stop criterion
  • Management support
  • Suspension or reversal plan

Assurance

  • Automated audit every sprint, run by the Data Governance Area
  • Verification of classification, audit trail and record sheet
  • Report to the committee at the end of each sprint
  • Scope limited to the artifact and the process, never the merits of the decision
  • The court's internal audit in the third line

Risk indicators and projections are preventive, since they act before quality loss sets in; reasoning sampling, the audit trail and anomaly flagging are detective. Conceptually aligned with the IIA Three Lines Model (2020).

Step 6: Integrated Dashboard and Augmented Intelligence

The trail created for auditing makes the oversight regime itself measurable. From it come, with no parallel data collection and no extra input from the operator, six indicators, one per step of the cycle: compliance (decision types with requirements met), classification (decision types classified by ISD with reassessment up to date), integrity (loads successfully validated), recovery (average time to fix failures), traceability (lineage mapped and accessible) and alerts (hit rate and false positives of predictive alerts, from the pilot of the predictive module). Reference goals are calibrated against the Phase 0 baseline. Measurement stops being a separate project and becomes a by-product of the record.

The same base supports the predictive opportunity. Court dashboards today are descriptive: they show what has already happened, without anticipating case backlog build-up or the risk of missing a goal. Projecting trends is the next step, and it depends on what comes before, because a predictive model over unvalidated data projects the error with more confidence. The judge or manager submits the projection to ISD scrutiny before acting.

Adequate reasoning in a sample and the usage indicators measured in Phase 0 (edit rate, time to confirmation and consultation of the source) reappear in the pilot as a benchmark, to show whether the controls produced the expected effect.

Integrated interface

The artifacts make up a single interface. Beside each indicator, the side panel shows the source and the data update, and the load control panel shows load validation. At the top, the alert card consolidates the unit's risk of quality loss and the projection of quality, volume and backlog up to the close of the period. Nothing on the screen blocks or conditions the decision. The committee sets the requirements and decides on exceptions, the Data Governance Area maintains the data and indicators, and management operates the interface.

Quality indicators, with qualitative goals and trend reading within each unit, never in a ranking, coexist with the quality-loss risk indicators, which flag deterioration before it sets in. The same dashboard is reused in the following sprints to monitor the pilot. Control is done by sprints, with a weekly reading of the preventive and usage indicators, a monthly trend of quality and risk and a projection updated every sprint.

Anticipated objections

The questions a governance committee asks first, and the answers the design already contains.

Question 1

Does this watch the civil servant or the judge?

Recording is by organizational unit, never by person. The trail documents technical execution and the unit acts, not the behavior of whoever decides.

Question 2

Does this interfere with the decision?

Nothing on the screen blocks, delays or steers the choice. The controls fall on the data, the sample and the unit's working conditions, and the reasoning required by art. 93, IX, of the Brazilian Constitution remains entirely with whoever decides.

Question 3

Does this slow the work down?

There is no additional step on the decision-maker's screen. The cost falls on the Data Governance Area and on management, and in the data steps automation reduces the time now spent on manual load checks.

Question 4

Why measure before acting?

Without a baseline, there is no way to know whether the controls worked or whether they were proportional to the problem. Phase 0 observes without intervening, and its results follow the rule of no individual metrics.
