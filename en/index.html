Decision Symbiosis

Data governance to prevent the loss of quality in dashboard-supported administrative decisions caused by excessive productivity demands. Instead of opening the technology black box, we govern the decision.

The committee's dilemma: every decision vs. volume To protect quality, the committee would have to assess the risk of every decision, which is impossible given the volume. The way out is to assess risk by decision type, with the probability of quality loss read by unit and period, and to bring only the exceptions to the committee.

Paradigm
From Artificial Intelligence to Augmented Intelligence: the machine extends human capacity, it does not replace it.
Focus
Decision Governance instead of attempting System Governance.
Scope
Technology-agnostic: applies to spreadsheets, traditional BI dashboards or Generative AI.
Method and Rule
Symbiosis is the working method; Governance is the validation rule.

The problem and the proposal

Court managers make decisions based on dashboards such as the PAI and e-Gestão, in administrative decisions such as reallocating the workforce, prioritizing tasks, organizing task forces and planning how to meet goals. The dashboards mostly show output that has already happened, and the goals they track are measured on fixed dates. The pilot is limited to these administrative decisions, and judicial decisions are outside this stage.

The central problem: excessive productivity demands. Volume goals with no quality counterweight push for ever shorter deadlines, and under time pressure judgment relies more on heuristics (Guthrie; Rachlinski; Wistrich, 2007). The risk is a loss of decision quality, not a lack of productivity.

The second problem: automation bias. Under pressure, managers tend to accept the dashboard figure as already verified, without checking the source or how recent the data is. Complacency with automation sets in whenever the tool replaces part of human verification, even in dashboards that only describe (Parasuraman; Manzey, 2010).

A hypothesis to confirm: quality loss at the end of the period. When measurement closes on fixed dates, effort tends to bunch up near the deadline, a pattern documented outside the Judiciary (Oyer, 1998). In a fictitious administrative office, the acts completed per week rise from 58 to 118 in the last week of the month, and rework climbs from 6% to 18%. At TRT-2 this pattern has not yet been measured, and a prior survey of e-Gestão and PAI data, followed by Phase 0, confirms or rules it out before any control is applied.

The committee's dilemma. To protect quality, the committee would have to assess the risk of every decision, which is impossible given the volume. Assessing only the system or the project, once, at approval, misses the fact that the same decision becomes riskier in the last week of the month. The way out is to assess risk by decision type, combining impact, which is stable, with the probability of quality loss, read by unit and period, and to bring only the exceptions to the committee.

The opportunity: governance before AI. As the largest labor court in Brazil, TRT-2 multiplies any data or decision error by its volume. CNJ Resolution 615/2025 requires risk classification, impact assessment, auditing and monitoring of AI solutions, and governing data and decisions before creating and using AI at scale costs less than fixing them afterwards.

What the pilot delivers. Three deliverables, in two units with administrative decisions supported by the PAI, and one rule that applies to all of them.

1. Governed data

Checks each dashboard load before releasing the next, restarts failures and shows, beside each indicator, where the data comes from and when it was updated. Indicator: validated loads and indicators with a visible source.

2. Risk by decision type

Classifies the administrative decision types supported by the dashboard, such as reallocating staff between units or organizing a task force, by impact and reversibility (ISD), and crosses the result with the unit's probability of quality loss. Indicator: types classified with reassessment up to date.

3. Weekly risk reading

Tracks, by unit and never by person, the preventive indicators and a simple trend up to the close of the period, and triggers management support, not enforcement. Indicator: alerts confirmed by the period's data.

No blocking on screen

The requirements fall on the data and the unit's management, not on the decision-maker's screen, and this applies equally to descriptive dashboards and, in the future, to AI solutions.

Decision Symbiosis

When a court adopts a technological system, the governance literature focuses exhaustively on trying to open the algorithm "black box". The problem is that, with Generative AI and complex models, full technical explainability is nearly impossible. The GSD-ISD model takes a pragmatic stance: instead of trying to govern the system, we should govern the decisions made with the system.

This approach removes the difficulty of technical explainability and reinforces human responsibility, in line with the legal nature of public administration. In a court, every decision requires legal grounds; technology is only the means, but final authorship is non-transferable and belongs exclusively to a human (judge, director or civil servant).

Technology Neutrality, Bias and Business Focus

Extending this model to traditional systems, such as Business Intelligence (BI) dashboards and decision support systems, is not only possible but strategic, for the following reasons:

  • Technology-agnostic governance: The court builds a future-proof framework. Whether the tool is ChatGPT, a predictive model, a Power BI dashboard or an Excel spreadsheet, the rule applies to the impact of the final decision. This avoids rewriting policies with every new innovation.
  • Mitigating automation bias in any environment: "Anchoring bias" is not exclusive to AI. A poorly built BI chart, or one fed with unvalidated data, can lead to a serious error. Governing the decision obliges the user to validate the information, however simple the source system may be.
  • Focus on the business, not only on IT: Models fail when they become an "IT checklist". Governing the decision places responsibility on the business unit that actually uses the dashboard, ensuring the system serves its practical purpose.

Augmented Intelligence: Symbiosis as the Engine

In this proposal, technology is not seen as a replacement but as Augmented Intelligence, that is, an extension of human capacity. The machine brings the raw power to process massive volumes of data, identify patterns or draft documents at scale; the human brings empathy, judgment, a sense of fairness and the legal seal of approval.

Decision Symbiosis is the method (how the work is done); decision governance is the framework that ensures the human has truly taken authorship, preventing the mere act of "pressing approve without reading".

The GSD-ISD model · specified and verified

Decision Sensitivity Index (ISD)

Classifies each decision type by risk, combining impact on rights and irreversibility. Oversight is applied to the decision and is proportional to risk.

Three control layers

Separates those who operate, those who govern and those who audit. In the pilot, the pilot team runs the automated checks every sprint on the classification, the record sheet and the integrity of the audit trail, the committee decides on exceptions, and the court's internal audit remains in the third line.

Impact indicators per project

Risk is assessed by decision type, and impact is measured by project. Every project that creates or changes a dashboard gets indicators on the decision types it supports, measured before and after: rework, revised decisions, missed deadlines, concentration at period end and the share of indicators with a known source, with average completion time as a counterweight.

Decision chain record

The Record Sheet logs the steps and the reasons, materializing the non-transferable human responsibility (legal seal of approval) required by the court regulatory framework.

Architecture extensions · under development and implemented

Preventive and predictive indicators under research

Context indicators anticipate quality loss, and a simple trend of the unit's weekly series signals the peak before the period closes, without blocking the decision-maker's screen.

Practicability and shadow AI under research

When the audited channel is overly bureaucratic, staff look for undeclared external solutions (loose spreadsheets, generic AI). Practicability is vital.

Data governance at the source implemented

A dashboard with a half-finished load leads to error. Validation between steps and deviation detection separate complete data from partial data at the BI source.

Tracking and goal control implemented

Each run of the pipeline receives a unique identifier, chained by an integrity check. The indicators compared with the historical pattern come from this trail. Trend projection is the next step, not yet implemented.

Prior survey and Phase 0 · baseline diagnosis, proposal not yet executed

Before setting requirements and thresholds, the proposed pilot measures how decisions are made today. Automation bias is documented, but its prevalence varies by context, and controls set without a diagnosis may be excessive or insufficient. For this reason, a prior survey of data already available and the two weeks of Phase 0 precede the 12-week pilot, organized in three sprints, and test the end-of-period concentration hypothesis before any control is applied.

Because the institutional audit trail does not capture the conduct of whoever decides, Phase 0 is a separate study, with informed consent and aggregated results.

The Decision Symbiosis cycle

After Phase 0, the four pillars above are organized into six steps, in which the human validates the system and governance validates the human. Step 6 reopens step 1: the cycle is iterative.

Step 1

Govern the use of the dashboard

The committee defines, by decision type, the requirements of each risk layer, combining probability and impact. The rule is recorded in the Record Sheet, and nothing on the screen blocks or conditions the decision.

Step 2

Calculate decision risk

Classifies the administrative decision types made from the dashboard by impact and reversibility (ISD) and crosses them with the unit's probability of quality loss, regardless of the technology adopted.

Step 3

Validate the data

Checks each load before releasing the next, restarts failures and detects deviations, mitigating bias caused by input errors.

Step 4

Document and Trace

Automatically records each run and chains it with an integrity check. Human intervention requires an identified person responsible and a reason. Every decision supported by the system has the signing person identified, with name and position; monitoring indicators, however, are by unit, never by person.

Step 5

Map the upstream process

Shows the data steps and earlier AI uses that produced the indicator, providing context for human judgment.

Step 6

Evaluate and Project (Predictive Dashboard)

Compares indicators with goals, uses a simple trend to project volume and rework up to the close of the period and the backlog for the following months, and anticipates risk by unit, subjecting the projection to the committee's scrutiny. Steps 1 to 5 are implemented as a proof of concept; the simple trend enters the pilot, and its usefulness is checked against confirmed alerts.

Governance, management and audit are distinct roles

Governance, exercised by the committee, sets the requirements for each layer, approves the thresholds, decides on exceptions and, at the end of each sprint, whether to expand, adjust or suspend. Management, that is, the business area and IT, operates the dashboard and the loads day to day, and responsibility for the decision lies with the business area that uses the dashboard, not with IT. Whoever sets the intensity of control is not whoever bears its cost. During the pilot, the pilot team runs the automated checks every sprint, which verify the risk classification, the integrity of the audit trail and the completeness of the record sheet, without examining the merits of the decision or the conduct of whoever decides. The court's internal audit unit remains in the third line of the Three Lines Model (IIA, 2020; CNJ Resolution 309/2020) and may use the trail as input without losing its independence.

Suggested follow-up: a data governance area

If the pilot confirms the problem and the usefulness of the weekly reading, the proposal suggests setting up a data governance area, sitting on the committee, to carry the work forward. It would be responsible for the quality, provenance and lineage of dashboard data, maintain the classification of decision types and the indicators, define with the requesting area the impact indicators of each project, and link project demands to the business area through data stewards appointed by the user areas (DAMA International, 2017), with the demand kept in the business and execution in IT. Creating the area is not part of this proposal and would depend on a decision by the court administration; the pilot results provide the basis for that decision.

Probability × impact and preventive and predictive indicators

The ISD measures the impact of the decision and its irreversibility. To it is added the probability of quality loss, rated low, medium or high by five context indicators tracked by unit, never by person: sustained deadline pressure, sudden volume acceleration, concentration of decisions at period end, load per workforce and backlog complexity, plus load validation and data freshness. Crossed with impact, the two dimensions form a risk matrix, which brings forward the reassessment of high-risk decision types in high-probability units. The predictive component is deliberately simple: a trend of the unit's own weekly series projected to the close of the period, with no complex model and no promised accuracy, whose usefulness is checked in the pilot itself by comparing the alerts with what actually happened. Outcome indicators already available, such as rework, revised decisions and missed deadlines, detect the loss after it occurs, with average completion time as a counterweight. Thresholds are calibrated in Phase 0, with no ranking between units, and the alert triggers support from management, not enforcement.

Impact indicators per project

Risk is assessed by decision type, and impact is measured by project. Every project that creates or changes a dashboard, and in the future an AI solution, receives on approval impact indicators on the decision types it supports, measured before and after: rework, revised decisions, missed deadlines, concentration of decisions at period end, share of indicators with a known source and, as a counterweight, average completion time. This way the committee knows whether the project improved the quality of decisions, and not only whether it was delivered.

Outside the pilot, for a later stage. Sampling of the reasoning behind decisions, usage metrics that would require changing national systems such as PJe (edit rate, time to confirmation and consultation of the source), and extension to judicial decisions are left for later.

Step 6: Integrated Dashboard and Augmented Intelligence

The trail created for auditing makes the oversight regime itself measurable. From it come, with no parallel data collection and no extra input from the operator, six indicators, one per step of the cycle: compliance (decision types with requirements met), classification (decision types classified by ISD with reassessment up to date), integrity (loads successfully validated), recovery (average time to fix failures), traceability (lineage mapped and accessible) and alerts (trend alerts confirmed by the period's data). Reference goals are calibrated against the Phase 0 baseline. Measurement stops being a separate project and becomes a by-product of the record.

The same base supports the predictive opportunity. Court dashboards today are descriptive: they show what has already happened, without anticipating case backlog build-up or the risk of missing a goal. Projecting trends is the next step, deliberately simple in the pilot, and it depends on what comes before, because a projection over unvalidated data projects the error with more confidence. The manager submits the projection to ISD scrutiny before acting.

Rework, revised decisions, missed deadlines and concentration at period end, measured in the baseline, reappear in the sprints as a benchmark, with average completion time as a counterweight, to show whether quality improves without slowing things down.

Integrated interface

The artifacts make up a single interface. Beside each indicator, the side panel shows the source and the data update, and the load control panel shows load validation. At the top, the alert card consolidates the unit's risk of quality loss and the trend in volume and rework up to the close of the period. Nothing on the screen blocks or conditions the decision. The committee sets the requirements and decides on exceptions, the pilot team maintains the data and indicators, and management operates the interface.

Quality indicators, with qualitative goals and trend reading within each unit, never in a ranking, coexist with the quality-loss risk indicators, which flag deterioration before it sets in. The same dashboard is reused in the following sprints to monitor the pilot. Control is done by sprints, with a weekly reading of the preventive indicators, a monthly trend of the outcome indicators and a projected trend reviewed every sprint.

Expected advantages

The pilot's focus is to measure the loss of quality in dashboard-supported administrative decisions. The advantages below are expected and will be confirmed by that measurement, with average completion time used as a counterweight.

Advantage 1

Less rework

Better decisions save the time now spent redoing tasks, revising decisions and recovering missed deadlines.

Advantage 2

Better spread of the workload across the month

By anticipating the closing peak, management redistributes the workload before the last week and reduces queues and bottlenecks.

Advantage 3

Less manual data checking

Automatic load validation and a visible data source save manual checks and avoid deciding on an outdated figure.

Advantage 4

No extra step for the decision-maker

Nothing blocks or delays the decision, and the cost of control falls on management, not on whoever decides.

Advantage 5

Quality without losing speed

Average completion time, measured before and after, shows whether quality improves without lengthening the process, in line with the right to a reasonable duration of proceedings (Brazilian Constitution, art. 5, LXXVIII).

Advantage 6

Quality risk anticipated

The weekly reading by unit, with no ranking, flags the week in which speed may cost quality, and the alert triggers support, not enforcement.

Advantage 7

Governance before AI

The classification of decision types and governed data prepare the court to use AI in compliance with CNJ Resolution 615/2025.

Advantage 8

Measurable effect per project

Each project's impact indicators make it possible to compare before and after, in the decisions the project supports.

Anticipated objections

The questions a governance committee asks first, and the answers the design already contains.

Question 1

Does this watch the civil servant?

No. Indicators are by organizational unit, with no individual metric and no content capture. The trail documents technical execution and the unit's acts, not the behavior of whoever decides.

Question 2

Does this interfere with the decision?

No. Nothing on the screen blocks, delays or steers the choice. The controls fall on the data and the unit's working conditions, and authorship and reasoning remain entirely with whoever decides.

Question 3

Does this slow the work down?

There is no additional step for the decision-maker. The cost falls on the pilot team and on management, and automatic load validation reduces the time now spent on manual checks.

Question 4

What if the end-of-month pattern does not show up?

That is what the prior survey and Phase 0 are for. Even without the pattern, governed data and the classification of decision types remain useful to prepare the court for AI, in compliance with CNJ Resolution 615/2025.

Question 5

Why measure before acting?

Without a baseline, there is no way to know whether the controls worked or whether they were proportional to the problem. Phase 0 observes without intervening, and its results follow the rule of no individual metrics.
